## Summary

HB 320 substantially restructured Chapter 72 (the Utah AI Policy Act) and introduced a second instrument type — the Joint Interpretation Agreement (JIA) — alongside the existing Regulatory Mitigation Agreement (RMA). Key changes:

- **Joint Interpretation Agreement (JIA) instrument.** A JIA is an agreement between a participant, OAIP, and a relevant agency or governmental entity that clarifies the application of a provision of state law or rule to AI technology (§13-72-101(12)). Unlike an RMA, which may waive or modify requirements, a JIA leaves the clarified requirements in force (§13-72-401(7)). The statute presents both agreements as ways OAIP may grant temporary regulatory mitigation (§13-72-401(2)) and does not describe reliance on a JIA.
- **Expanded counterparties.** Agreements may now involve the judiciary, state-funded higher or public education institutions, and political subdivisions — not only executive-branch agencies (§13-72-101(10), §13-72-401).
- **Extended term.** The maximum agreement term was extended from 12 months plus a single 12-month extension (24 months) to 12 months plus up to two 12-month extensions — up to 36 months total (§13-72-403).
- **Mandatory audits.** OAIP "shall perform regular audits" while an agreement is active (§13-72-401(6)).
- **Eligibility prongs carried over and extended to JIAs.** The five prongs SB 149 enacted for regulatory mitigation now apply to both RMAs and JIAs: technical expertise and capability, financial resources to meet obligations during testing, potential substantial consumer benefits that may outweigh identified risks, a risk-monitoring plan, and appropriately limited testing (§13-72-402). HB 320 renumbered them and broadened prong (a) to cover implementing and using or supervising use.

The restructure formalizes Utah's two-instrument architecture — RMA (waiver) and JIA (interpretation) — that PubLedge uses as the prototypical pattern for fact-specific written interpretations between a regulator and a regulated party.

### Talking Point

> "Utah HB 320 (2026) gave Utah's Office of AI Policy a second instrument: the Joint Interpretation Agreement — an agreement that clarifies how existing state law or rule applies to AI technology, without the law being waived. That two-instrument architecture (waiver + interpretation) is the pattern PubLedge formalizes for the cross-jurisdictional registry."
