## Summary

DOPL forgoes unlawful-conduct and unprofessional-conduct enforcement under §58-1-501(1)-(2) and related professional-licensing provisions against any provider who (1) acts solely as the named prescriber for AI-authorized renewals and (2) does not interact directly with a patient or other provider. Legion §16(D) adds a condition that the provider abides by the agreement and any agreement with the participant; Doctronic §15(D) states no such condition. The covered chapter provisions differ by agreement. Forbearance applies only to conduct authorized by the agreement.

## What Counts

- Provider's role is limited to being the named prescriber for AI-generated renewal
- Provider does not interact directly with the patient or another provider in the renewal chain
- Provider acts in reliance on the participant's AI technology
- Provider abides by the terms of the RMA and any separate agreement with the participant (Legion §16(D) condition; not stated in Doctronic §15(D))
- Doctronic §15(D)(2) chapter provisions, as written in the signed text: 58-31b-502, 58-60-110, 58-61-502, 59-67-502 [sic], 58-68-502, 59-70a-503 [sic], and 58-71-502
- Legion §16(D)(2) chapter provisions: 58-31b-501-503, 58-67-501-503, and 58-68-501-503 (the broader list adding 58-60, 58-61 and 58-70a is in §16(E), which covers supporting-services providers, not named prescribers)

## What Does Not Count

- Providers who interact directly with patients or other providers
- Conduct beyond the authorized renewal workflow
- Conduct inconsistent with agreement terms
- Enforcement under statutes not enumerated in the agreement's mitigation section

## Statute Anchors

- Utah Code §58-1-501(1) and (2) — Unlawful and unprofessional conduct baseline
- UAC R156-1-501(2)(b) — Administrative rule
- Utah RMAs 2025-002 (Doctronic) §15(D) and 2026-001 (Legion) §16(D) (supporting-services forbearance is separate, §16(E))
