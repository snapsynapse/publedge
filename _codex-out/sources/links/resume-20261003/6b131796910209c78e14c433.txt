NEWS IMAGE SIZING - the usual cause of an ugly crop. Each slot is a FIXED 4:3 box filled with object-fit:cover, which scales the photo until it covers the box and then trims the overhang from the EDGES, keeping the centre. A 16:9 photo loses about a quarter of its width; a portrait photo loses most of its height. Crop to 4:3 before uploading. Aspect ratio exactly 4:3 (1.333), one of the four ratios the design system recommends. Recommended 1200 x 900 px. Renders at 340 x 255 on desktop and up to 420 x 315 on a phone, covering a 2x screen. Minimum 800 x 600 px. Maximum useful 1600 x 1200 px; larger only costs load time. Safe area keep the subject centred and leave about 10% clear on all four sides. No face, logo or text at an edge. To change a slot's ratio, edit aspect-ratio on .oaip-slide-img AND both the width and height attributes on that slide's . Change all three together or the browser reserves the wrong space and the page shifts as images load. Our job is to keep Utah’s laws and rules current with a technology that is changing quickly. We study how artificial intelligence is actually being used in Utah, we work directly with the companies and institutions building it, and we recommend changes to state law and rules based on what we learn. We also run a supervised AI Sandbox program that lets a company test a new AI product here under agreed conditions and close oversight. Observe and learn We research how AI is being used, what it does well, and where it goes wrong. Then publish what we find so lawmakers and the public can see it. Protect the public Every company in our program agrees to limits on how far it can go, safeguards it must keep in place, and disclosures it must make to the people using its product. We audit them while their agreement is active. Foster innovation Instead of guessing whether old rules prohibit new technology, a company can work it out with us and the relevant agency in the open and on the record for a set period of time. What our AI sandbox allows. Utah’s AI Sandbox lets a company enter an agreement with this office and the agency that regulates it. An agreement can adjust how a specific rule applies during a limited test period, but the company stays subject to every other legal requirement, and being accepted is not an endorsement or approval of the product by the State of Utah.
